For first-time visitors

What Is Durga Puja?

Durga Puja is an autumn festival honouring the goddess Durga's victory over the buffalo-demon Mahishasura: good over evil, and a celebration of the divine feminine. Bengalis have gathered for it for centuries.

In Kolkata it grew into something larger. Each neighbourhood committee raises a temporary pavilion, a pandal, and installs the goddess inside it. Some pandals are traditional shrines. Others are full-scale replicas of temples and monuments, or immersive installations built by professional theme-makers. More than 3,000 appear across Kolkata and its suburbs every year.

In December 2021 UNESCO inscribed Durga Puja in Kolkata on its list of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, recognising how a whole city turns into a public art event.

Ephemeral architecture

Pandals are designed like installations and built in a matter of weeks, then taken down once the festival ends.

Living ritual

Priests, drummers and families keep the rites going for days, from the awakening of the goddess to the farewell at the river.

The pandal-trail table

Phuchka, egg rolls, jhaal muri and Kolkata biryani. Eating is as much a part of the festival as the pandals themselves.

A city out at night

After dark the streets fill and families walk from pandal to pandal. The crowd is a big part of the atmosphere.

The Five Sacred Days

Five days, five moods. The festival builds from a quiet awakening at dusk to a farewell at the river. Choose a day to see what happens.

Day I of V

The Awakening

The festival opens. Before the goddess can be worshipped she has to be awakened. This is Akal Bodhon, the out-of-season invocation that Lord Rama is said to have made before his battle with Ravana. As evening falls the priests call her into the idol, and most pandals open to the public.

  • Kalparambha and Akal Bodhon: the ritual invocation of the goddess into the idol
  • Amantran and Adhivas: the formal invitation and settling of the deity
  • Pandals light up and open, with lighter crowds than the nights that follow
Best for settling in. Walk one cluster in the early evening and see the festival before its peak nights.
Day II of V

The First Full Day

The first full day of worship. At sunrise a banana plant dressed as a bride, the Nabapatrika, is bathed in the river and installed beside the idol. It stands for nine plant forms of the goddess. From here the daily Anjali offerings begin and the streets start to fill.

  • Kola Bou: the ritual bathing of the Nabapatrika, at the river at dawn
  • Idols are fully installed and the pandals stay open through the day
  • A Sunday this year, so expect the city out in force by the afternoon
Best for the morning. Calm pandals, the best light for photography, and the river ritual at dawn.
Peak day
Day III of V

The Sacred Peak

The heart of the festival. Devotees offer flowers at the pandals in Pushpanjali, a young girl is worshipped as the living goddess in Kumari Puja, and at the meeting of Ashtami and Navami comes Sandhi Puja: 48 minutes of 108 lamps, 108 lotus flowers and unbroken drumming.

  • Pushpanjali: communal flower offerings to the goddess
  • Kumari Puja: a girl worshipped as the goddess herself
  • Sandhi Puja: the hour is set by the panchang, so we confirm it a few days ahead
  • The night crowds are among the largest of the festival
Choose your intensity. Sandhi Puja for the most charged moment of the festival, or the morning for a calmer view.
Day IV of V

Drums and Smoke

The last day of worship. Maha Aarti in the evening, and the dhunuchi naach: dancers carry burning clay pots of coconut husk and incense, moving to the beat of the dhak until the pandal fills with smoke.

  • Navami Homa, the fire ritual, and Maha Aarti
  • Dhunuchi naach at the larger pandals, best seen in the evening
  • The last big night before the farewell
Learn the steps first. Our Immersion and Connoisseur journeys include a private dhunuchi lesson, so you know what you are watching.
Day V of V

The Farewell

Vijaya Dashami. Married women apply vermilion to the goddess and to one another in Sindur Khela, sweets are shared, and then the idols are carried in procession to the Hooghly. Made of river clay, she goes back to the river.

  • Sindur Khela: vermilion, sweets and laughter at the pandals
  • Visarjan: processions to the ghats along the Hooghly
  • Bijoya greetings follow, with visits to elders and shared sweets in the days after
Included in every journey. Watching the immersion from a private ghat is part of all three of our journeys.

Dates follow the Kolkata panchang as published by Drik Panchang. Ritual hours can shift by a few hours, and we confirm them with committees closer to each day.

Day by day

What a Puja Day Actually Looks Like

Durga Puja is not a list of tickets to collect. It has a rhythm: calm mornings, a long rest, then a city that stays awake. This is a sample Saptami, Sunday 18 October, shaped around that rhythm.

A sample, not a fixed itinerary. We adjust for crowds, road closures, weather and the panchang.

Dawn

Kola Bou at the river

We meet before sunrise at a ghat on the Hooghly for the ritual bathing of the Nabapatrika. The air is cool, the crowds are thin, and the light is the kind photographers wait all year for.

Mid-morning

The calm hours in North Kolkata

Pandals are quiet and you can actually study the craftsmanship. We walk the old-city cluster around Bagbazar and Kumartuli while the streets still belong to walkers.

Lunch

A proper Bengali thali

Out of the heat, seated, and fed well. The night circuit runs on street food, so this is the one full sit-down meal of the day.

Afternoon

Rest, on purpose

Back to the hotel through the warmest hours. From around seven the crowds surge, and you will want the energy.

Early evening

South Kolkata as the lights come on

One cluster per evening, never the whole city in a night. We work the Kalighat and Deshapriya Park circuit, by car where the roads are open and on foot where they are not.

Night

Street food and the surge

Phuchka, egg rolls and jhaal muri between pandals, in the crowd that makes Kolkata's Puja what it is. Big pandals often send men and women in through separate queues, so we agree a meeting point before every entry.

Late

Home by ten, or not at all

Families and older guests head back around ten. If you want the all-night version, we stay out with you.

The Beginning

The Dawn of Mahalaya

Long before first light touches the Ganges, Kolkata awakens. The air carries Birendra Krishna Bhadra's immortal voice — a chant that has stirred Bengali souls for nearly a century. This is Mahalaya, the sacred dawn that summons Devi Durga from the heavens down to earth.

We take you to the riverbanks at 4 in the morning, wrapped in the cold October mist, to witness the first offerings cast upon the sacred water. A moment that exists nowhere else on earth — included exclusively in The Connoisseur.

The Creation

Born from Sacred Clay

In the ancient potters' quarter of Kumartuli, master sculptors transform Ganges mud, straw, and sacred clay into the form of the Goddess herself. This tradition is centuries old — passed through families, each idol a singular work of devotional art that will live for five days before returning to the river.

We bring you into the ateliers before the city wakes — a private encounter with the artisans, their hands deep in clay, breathing the divine into form.

The Pandals

Where Art Meets the Sacred

Every year, hundreds of neighbourhood committees transform Kolkata into the world's greatest open-air museum of ephemeral art. These temporary temples rival the finest contemporary installations anywhere on earth. We grant you private access to the most extraordinary ones — curated, guided, and unhurried — before the millions arrive.

Sindur Khela

A Farewell in Vermillion

On the final day, married Bengali women gather at the pandals in their finest red-bordered saris. They anoint the Goddess with sindur — sacred vermillion — and then turn to each other, smearing each other's faces and hair in a joyful, tearful celebration of womanhood, love, and impending separation.

It is simultaneously the most joyous and the most melancholic moment in the Bengali calendar. You will witness it from within, not from outside the gate.

Dhunuchi Naach

The Dance of Sacred Smoke

As the drums reach their crescendo, devotees step forward clutching clay pots of smouldering coconut husks and incense — dhunuchi. Thick white smoke billows into the night. The dancer enters a trance, swaying and spinning in slow devotion before the face of the Goddess.

We arrange a private session with a master dhunuchi dancer who shares the spiritual meaning — and teaches you the first steps of this ancient devotional form.

Bisarjan

Her Return to the Eternal

On the final evening, the entire city converges on the Hooghly. Thousands of idols are borne through weeping, dancing crowds into the sacred river. The Goddess dissolves back into the water as the city holds its collective breath, then erupts into a grief that is also a profound celebration. You will stand on a private ghat as she goes home.

Where the Pandals Are

Kolkata's Puja falls into clusters. We give each cluster its own block of time, so you see the best of a district without zig-zagging across the city.

Circuit one

North Kolkata

Heritage and tradition
  • Bagbazar SarbojaninRegarded as Kolkata's first true community puja (1910). Deliberately traditional, and close to the river.
  • Kumartuli ParkBeside the idol-makers' quarter, with classical themes and music.
  • College SquareRunning since 1948 beside a lake, known for lighting that reflects in the water.
  • Sreebhumi Sporting ClubLake Town's headline-grabber, known for life-size temple and monument replicas.
Typical metro spine: Sovabazar-Sutanuti, Shyambazar, Belgachia
Circuit two

Central Kolkata

The bridge between the clusters
  • Santosh Mitra SquareSince 1936, one of the city's most historic and consistently elaborate committees.
  • Mohammad Ali ParkSince 1969, famous for grand replicas of monuments from around the world.
Good to know: a convenient stopover between the North and South circuits
Circuit three

South Kolkata

Themes and glamour
  • Ekdalia EvergreenRecreates a major Indian temple each year, with very tall idols.

Typical metro belt: Kalighat to Deshapriya Park

Our Story

Durga Puja Kolkata began with a simple frustration: watching visitors arrive for the world's greatest festival and leave having seen it only from the far side of a barricade — a spectacle glimpsed between someone else's shoulders.

We grew up inside this festival — not as tour operators, but as Kolkata natives for whom Ashtami evenings and Kumartuli's clay-scented lanes were simply home. The relationships that make a different kind of journey possible are ones we've held personally, long before we thought to share them with anyone else.

October 2026 is our founding season. We've built this practice deliberately small from the ground up — because the moment a group grows past a handful of people, the doors that matter start to close.

Visa

Most visitors need an India e-Visa, applied for online on the Indian government's official portal (indianvisaonline.gov.in). Fees and validity depend on your nationality and the visa type, and your passport should be valid for at least six months on arrival. With the festival close, apply as soon as your dates are firm rather than in the last week. We are happy to walk you through it.

Flights and arrival

Kolkata's airport is Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose International (CCU). Most travellers from North America and Europe connect through a Gulf, Southeast Asian or Indian hub, so allow for a long travel day. Private transfers are included in every journey.

When to arrive

The Essence begins on 17 October, The Immersion on 14 October and The Connoisseur on 10 October. Whichever you choose, we recommend landing a day early to recover from jet lag, because Shashthi evening is not the moment to be exhausted. Ask us about adding the extra night.

What to wear and pack
  • Mid-October is warm and humid, roughly 24 to 32°C, with a chance of light rain as the monsoon fades.
  • Comfortable, stable footwear matters most. Expect kilometres of walking on uneven ground.
  • Breathable cotton by day and a light layer for early mornings. Skip heavy embroidery and velvet.
Crowds, safety and comfort

Mornings and early afternoons are calm, and from about seven in the evening the crowds surge, peaking on Saptami, Ashtami and Navami nights. We pace every day around that rhythm. Kolkata Police increase their presence during the festival and run women's help desks (the emergency number is 100, and the women's helpline is 1091). We agree a meeting point before entering any large pandal.

Accessibility, families and older guests

A published accessibility protocol covers 24 prominent pandals, addressing ramps, accessible toilets and signage, and some pandals have first-aid points with wheelchairs. Tell us about mobility needs or young children and we will weight the itinerary towards daytime and the more accessible pandals.
